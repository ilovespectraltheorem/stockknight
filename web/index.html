            Maki in two minutes

            The rules Stockhorse and every part of this site play by (ruleset knightchess-2).

            Board and pieces

            • 6×6 board. White sets up on rank 1 as bishop, knight, queen, king, knight, rook, with pawns on rank 2. Black's setup is the same rotated half a turn.
            • King and queen both step one square in any direction. No castling.
            • Rook, bishop and knight move as in chess.
            • A king attacks the 8 squares around it, as in chess, so the two kings can never stand next to each other.

            Pawns

            • A pawn can always move or capture one square straight ahead, all the way to the far rank. It never captures diagonally.
            • In the opponent's half (ranks 4–6 for White) it can also move or capture one square left or right.
            • A pawn never moves backwards. No double step, en passant or promotion: a pawn on the far rank can only step sideways.

            Drops

            • Each side starts with 2 pawns, 1 rook and 1 bishop in reserve. Instead of moving, you may drop one on any empty square.
            • Captured pieces leave the game; they never join a reserve.
            • No pawn drop on a file that already has one of your own pawns; any rank is fine, the far rank included. A rook or bishop drop needs fewer than 2 of your rooks and bishops on the board.

            Winning

            • If you have no legal move, you lose. Checkmate and stalemate both lose.
            • Draws: the same position three times, or bare kings with empty reserves.

            A king and one or two minor pieces against a bare king

            • A lone bishop or a lone knight can never give checkmate: the piece that gives check never guards the squares directly beside the king (up, down, left and right), and the other king cannot guard all of those without standing next to it.
            • Two knights can checkmate, but cannot force it: the bare king can always step away from a mate.
            • All three still win, because stalemate loses. With the stronger side to move, king and bishop always win (at most 20 moves), king and knight win unless the knight is already trapped (at most 33 moves), and king and two knights always win (at most 29 moves).
            • The analysis board knows every such position exactly: set one up, or pick one from Lone-piece endings, to see the result of every move.

            To drop, click a piece in your reserve (beside the board) and then an empty square.
